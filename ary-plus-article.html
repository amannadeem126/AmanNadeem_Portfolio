ARY Plus Is Taking Over Pakistani Dramas. Here Is What That Means for You

ARY Digital has moved its dramas off YouTube for viewers in Pakistan. Before you download anything, it helps to understand why, and what to watch out for.

By Aman NadeemOctober 2026 · 6 min read

If you have spent any evening in the last few weeks hunting for the latest episode of an ARY drama on YouTube, you already know something has changed. The episodes you used to find in two taps are gone, and social media has been loudly busy ever since. The reason has a name: ARY Plus.

Every few years, Pakistani viewers get a reminder that the way we watch is never settled. First, it was cable and the prime-time slot. Then YouTube turned every phone into a television. Now, one of the nation's largest entertainment networks is calling us to download its own app instead. What is really going on, why it matters, and how to switch without falling for a scam.

So, what actually changed?

According to news reports, ARY Digital stopped streaming its premium dramas on YouTube for viewers in Pakistan from 17 September 2026, and now points them to ARY Plus, its own streaming app. Shows like Tamasha are part of the same push. Outside the country, the picture is different. Reports say the series remain free on YouTube for overseas audiences, so this is a local shift rather than a worldwide disappearance.

The app is free to download and, at the time of writing, free to watch, supported by ads. One paid extra has been reported: the uncut live stream of Tamasha, priced at Rs 100 in Pakistan and $1 abroad.

ARY has also shared some early numbers. Downloads reportedly crossed 10.8 million before the official launch, with an average viewing session of about 25 minutes. Downloads are not the same thing as loyal viewers, but a figure like that tells you plenty of people were curious enough to try.

Why would a network walk away from YouTube?

On paper, it sounds almost reckless. YouTube is free hosting, enormous reach, and already installed on nearly every phone in the country. So why leave?

Control, mostly. When you stream on your own app, you decide how ads are sold, how episodes are released and what you learn about your audience. On YouTube, a big slice of all that belongs to the platform. Dawn's coverage points to exactly this: more command over local advertising, distribution and user data, at the cost of splitting the audience between two services.

If viewers know where to find you, you no longer have to rent your audience from someone else.

ARY's CEO, Salman Iqbal, has described the app as a project years in the making, with the goal of taking Pakistani dramas to a global audience. He has also talked about letting independent creators upload their own content and invited rival networks to join in. Whether they will is another story.

The viewer's side of the story

Nobody asked us if we wanted another app. You can see it in the comments, where a fair number of people say they'd sooner skip the dramas altogether than install one more thing. Others are just worried it won't feel like YouTube, where speed controls, playlists and "continue watching" already work the way your thumb expects.

Honestly, they have a point. Storage fills up fast, data packages run out, and a lot of people are still on older phones. So the app is going to be judged on boring stuff. Does it open quickly? Does it freeze at 9 pm when half the country is watching? How many ads land in the middle of a big scene? Can you find last week's episode without hunting for it?

There is a flip side, though. An app can offer things YouTube never will, like proper episode tracking or early and exclusive episodes, if ARY actually builds them. That, more than anything, will decide whether people stay.

Safety checklist

Before you download anything

This is the part I care about most as a software engineer. Whenever an app suddenly becomes the only way to watch something popular, fake versions appear within days. Search for "ARY Plus APK" and you will probably find sites promising a "premium unlocked" or "ad-free" build. Don't bother with them. Once a stranger has edited a file, you can't tell what got added, and the permissions screen is usually where it shows.

Some quick habits help. Get the app from your phone's official store and look at the publisher's name, because lookalikes with one letter changed do exist. Treat any "ad-free" version with suspicion, since ads are how the app earns its keep. And if a drama app suddenly wants to read your messages or contacts, that's a no.

My take

I think ARY is making a reasonable bet that carries real risk. The upside is a platform it fully owns. The downside is splitting its audience at exactly the moment audiences hate switching. In the end it will probably come down to the unglamorous stuff: steady streaming, a sensible number of ads, and a library people can actually browse.

There is also a bigger question hanging over the industry. If ARY Plus holds on to its viewers, other Pakistani networks will be watching closely, and it would not be surprising to see rivals consider the same move. I'm guessing here, to be clear. But here is why I think this is bigger than one app: if every channel launches its own, you will end up with five logins for what used to be one tab.

So, what should you do? If you follow ARY dramas, get the official app and give it a few days before you judge it. Remember that reports call it free "for now," and that can change. And if it annoys you, say so loudly. Networks notice when the numbers drop.

Whatever happens next, my advice doesn't change much: official sources only, read the permissions, and keep the drama on the screen.
